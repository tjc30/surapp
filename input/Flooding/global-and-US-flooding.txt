```python
import pandas as pd
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
import numpy as np

# Create representative decadal data based on EPA, NOAA, and EM-DAT reports (1950 - 2020)
decades = [1950, 1960, 1970, 1980, 1990, 2000, 2010, 2020]

# US High Tide Flooding (Average days per year, National Median based on NOAA)
us_coastal_flooding_days = [0.5, 0.8, 1.2, 1.8, 2.3, 2.8, 4.0, 5.0]

# US Extreme Precipitation (Percentage of land area experiencing extreme 1-day events, based on EPA)
us_heavy_precip_index = [8.2, 8.5, 8.8, 9.4, 10.5, 11.2, 11.8, 12.5]

# Global Flood Events (Reported average annual events per decade, based on EM-DAT trend approximation)
global_flood_events = [15, 25, 45, 75, 120, 155, 165, 175]

df = pd.DataFrame({
'Year': decades,
'US_Coastal_Flood_Days': us_coastal_flooding_days,
'US_Heavy_Precip_Area_Pct': us_heavy_precip_index,
'Global_Reported_Flood_Events': global_flood_events
})

fig, ax1 = plt.subplots(figsize=(10, 6))

color = 'tab:blue'
ax1.set_xlabel('Decade')
ax1.set_ylabel('US Coastal Flood Days & Precip Area (%)', color=color)
l1, = ax1.plot(df['Year'], df['US_Coastal_Flood_Days'], color='tab:cyan', marker='o', linewidth=2, label='US Coastal
Flood Days (NOAA)')
l2, = ax1.plot(df['Year'], df['US_Heavy_Precip_Area_Pct'], color='tab:blue', marker='s', linewidth=2, label='US Extreme
Precip Area % (EPA)')
ax1.tick_params(axis='y', labelcolor=color)

ax2 = ax1.twinx()
color = 'tab:red'
ax2.set_ylabel('Global Reported Flood Events per Year', color=color)
l3, = ax2.plot(df['Year'], df['Global_Reported_Flood_Events'], color=color, marker='^', linewidth=2, label='Global Flood
Events (EM-DAT)')
ax2.tick_params(axis='y', labelcolor=color)

# Add legends
lines = [l1, l2, l3]
labels = [l.get_label() for l in lines]
ax1.legend(lines, labels, loc='upper left')

plt.title('Global & US Flooding Trends (1950 - 2020)')
plt.grid(True, alpha=0.3)
plt.tight_layout()
plt.show()

# Show dataset summary
summary = df.describe()
print(summary)
```

### Key Trends for Your Dashboard

**1. US Coastal Flooding (Ocean-Driven)**
Driven by a global sea-level rise of roughly 8 to 9 inches since 1880, "high tide" or nuisance flooding has increased 5-
to 10-fold since the 1960s across several US coastal cities [1.3.1, 1.3.3]. The national annual frequency of high tide
flooding reached an average of four days in 2022, up from nearly zero in the mid-20th century [1.3.3]. Regions like the
Southeast Atlantic and Gulf Coasts have seen a 400% to 1,100% increase just since the year 2000 [1.3.2].

**2. US Heavy Precipitation (Storm-Driven)**
Warmer air holds more moisture (roughly 4% more per 1°F of warming), which fuels heavier inland downpours [1.2.2].
According to the EPA, nine of the top ten years for extreme one-day precipitation events in the contiguous US have
occurred since 1990 [2.2.1]. Between 1958 and 2021, the heaviest rainfall events became significantly wetter across all
major regions, led by the Southeast (+37%) and the Northern Rockies/Plains (+24%) [1.2.2].

**3. Global Flood Disasters**
Data aggregated from the Emergency Events Database (EM-DAT) highlights a massive global uptick in reported
weather-related disasters. While partly influenced by improved global reporting systems, the frequency of major
international flood events grew from an average of roughly 15 to 20 recorded events per year in the 1950s to well over
150 per year in the most recent decade [2.1.2, 2.1.3].
